# Writing standards — academic register

Applies to everything that leaves the system under Nikkhil's name: proposals, letters, CVs,
research statements, outreach. It does **not** govern the system's own instruction files or working
notes. Where the humanizer skill (`D:/Resume/Agent Skill/humanizer/SKILL.md`) conflicts with this
file, this file wins.

## Standing rules
- **No em dashes.** Hyphens or a restructured sentence.
- **No rule-of-three padding** ("clear, concise, and compelling").
- **No significance inflation** — cut "crucial", "pivotal", "vital", "testament". Let the
  contribution carry itself.
- **Never casualise.** Formality is correct here. Do not apply the humanizer's "Personality and
  Soul" section to any formal document: no conversational asides, no jokes, no deliberate mess.
- **Name the specific thing** — the framework, dataset, method, statute, figure.

## Register by document
- **Proposal** — scholarly and precise. Argue from evidence, hedge appropriately ("the evidence
  suggests", not "this proves"), define constructs, acknowledge limitations. Every empirical claim
  is citable and carries a verification status (`phd-system/methods/verification.md`).
- **Motivation letter** — formal, specific, first person allowed but measured. Three jobs: why this
  research, why this group by name, why Nikkhil is ready. Address gaps with poise, never confession.
- **CV** — achievement-led, no first-person pronouns, action-verb openers.
- **Outreach** — short, specific, framed as a contribution rather than a request for a favour.

## Bullet formula
`Action verb + what was done + scope/method/tools + concrete outcome.`
Vary openers; no verb opens more than two bullets. Avoid "helped", "worked on", "was responsible
for", "handled".

to this call's language · register intact · limitations acknowledged (proposals).
