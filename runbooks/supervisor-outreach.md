# Runbook — Supervisors: discovery, digest, outreach

Steps 1-3 are mechanical and may be delegated to `phd-research`; the drafting and the send decision
stay in the main session.

## 1. Discover
Faculty directory, Google Scholar, recent group publications. Per candidate supervisor capture:
name, title, group, two or three representative recent papers, themes, and whether they are
accepting doctoral students or lead the advertised position.

## 2. Digest
For the top one or two, run **corpus mode** of `phd-system/methods/literature-review.md`: resolve
the person's identity first, collect the reachable publication list to
`programs/<Programme>/research/<slug>-publications.md`, then map three to five research
trajectories across the corpus and the papers that most changed its direction. Rank by contrastive
originality and method, not by prestige or citation count.

The output is a brief on where Nikkhil's question connects to a *trajectory* rather than to one
recent title. Read the actual papers; do not characterise work from an abstract fragment. State the
gaps in the publication list instead of filling them by inference.
→ CHECKPOINT: confirm the target supervisor and the connection angle.

## 3. Derive the email
Find a public `name@institution` address to infer the pattern, then apply it. Confidence: **high**
(listed on the faculty page or pattern confirmed from a real address), **medium** (inferred), **low**
(guessed). Prefer an official listed address. Label honestly — never call a guess high.

## 4. Verify before writing
Run the people-and-institutions check in `phd-system/methods/verification.md`: current rank and title, current
institution, whether they still supervise there, whether the team described exists, and full author
lists on anything cited back to them. Misstating a supervisor's own work to their face is the
costliest error available here. A single Hamburg pass produced ten corrections.

## 5. Write `supervisors.md`
Per person: name · title · group · email with confidence and source · representative papers ·
one-line why-fit · accepting students · deadline.

## 6. Draft the message
Short, specific, in the call's language:
- one line on who Nikkhil is;
- two or three sentences connecting *their* specific research to his question — this is the whole
  point, generic emails fail;
- a concrete ask, and a pointer to the attached proposal or CV.

Frame as a contribution, not "I would appreciate the opportunity". No fabricated familiarity. One
tailored message per supervisor, never a mass send.

**Optional second deliverable — the pre-application fit inquiry.** Before applying, a separate
short email asking whether the proposed direction is something they would be interested in
supervising. Keep it distinct from the formal application courtesy note. This has worked: van
Veelen confirmed the Lund angle "very much fits" and supplied the extended project description; van
der Zee endorsed the Hamburg direction.

→ CHECKPOINT: present `supervisors.md` and the draft. **Never send. Nikkhil sends.**
