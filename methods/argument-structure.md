# Method — Argument structure (adapted from Feynman `paper-narrative`)

Works on what a document *argues*, not on how it reads. Use it on a proposal draft that is well
written and still unconvincing, on a motivation letter that lists rather than builds, and when a
supervisor's feedback is that the pieces do not add up. It runs before the register pass, never
instead of it: `phd-system/profile/writing-standards.md` governs the prose, this governs the load
path.

The output is an evidence arc, not a writing checklist. If the result could apply to any document
in the field, it has not done the job.

## Steps
1. **Extract the spine.** The single central claim, in one sentence. The reader it is for — this
   committee, this supervisor. The evidence chain that carries the claim, link by link. And for
   each section, the decision it exists to let the reader make. A section that supports no decision
   is a candidate for cutting no matter how good it is.
2. **Find where the load is not carried.** A section claiming more than its evidence proves. A
   method assumed rather than specified. A step where the argument jumps. The gap between what the
   research question asks and what the design can actually answer — in a PhD proposal this is the
   most common structural break and the one a committee finds fastest.
3. **Reorder, merge, split, cut — by argument.** Not by symmetry, not by length. A proposal usually
   improves when the contribution moves earlier and the literature narrows to what the question
   needs. Cutting a strong paragraph that carries no load is the normal outcome of this method, not
   a sign it went wrong.
4. **Check the arc against the target.** Does the sequence answer the call's stated priorities in
   the call's own order? Does the group's work appear where it matters to the argument rather than
   deposited in one paragraph of flattery?
5. **Write a revision plan.** Ordered, concrete, one item per structural break, each naming the
   section it touches and what it changes. Save to
   `programs/<Programme>/research/<slug>-structure.md`.

Structural surgery on a proposal changes what Nikkhil is claiming, so the revision plan takes a
**→ CHECKPOINT** before it is applied. The method proposes an arc; it never selects the research
question, which is canon rule 1 and holds here without exception.
