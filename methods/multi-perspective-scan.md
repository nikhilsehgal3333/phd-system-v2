# Method — Multi-perspective scan (optional)

Fast divergence and stress-testing across several expert lenses. Use for proposal framing and
gap-finding, borderline fit calls, reading a group's positioning, and pre-submission stress tests.
Skip it for mechanical work and wherever a single verified source is what is actually needed. It is
token-heavy — reserve it for flagship targets.

**Two hard limits:** its output never selects Nikkhil's research question (it produces options he
confirms), and no persona may invent his experience or credentials. Everything it produces is a
hypothesis that must pass `phd-system/methods/verification.md` before entering a deliverable. Personas share
the same model priors, so their agreement is not independent confirmation.

## Steps
1. **Scan.** Pick a concrete target and simulate five lenses. Research cast: practitioner ·
   academic · skeptic · economist · historian. Proposal-framing cast: target supervisor ·
   methodologist · skeptical examiner · policy stakeholder · field historian. Fit cast: hiring
   committee · skeptical reviewer · the ideal rival candidate · the funder · a past successful
   applicant. Per lens: core position, strongest claimed support (flagged unverified), and the one
   thing only this lens would say.
2. **Contradiction map.** Where lenses clash and on what claims; the question that would resolve the
   biggest clash; and **what no lens addressed** — the blind spot, usually the most valuable output.
3. **Synthesis.** A short briefing, three to five findings ranked by reliability, one non-obvious
   connection, and candidate directions framed as options.
4. **Self peer-review.** Confidence per finding (low scores become verification tasks), the weakest
   link and what evidence would settle it, which lens dominated, and what a domain examiner would
   say to fix.

Save runs to `programs/<Programme>/perspectives_<topic>.md` as a working note. Carry forward only
the verified gap into the framing, and the low-confidence findings into the verification queue.
